What “Single Source of Truth” Means

Use this reference to design a registry for your organization. The registry itself lives in your approved systems.

Give every record an authoritative home and a stable way to find it. A central registry can point to multiple source systems and record ownership, access, and lifecycle without storing copies of their contents.

  • Identify the current source, owner, and version for each object.
  • Link related records and artifacts by stable ID.
  • Give teams and reviewers a shared reference for migrations, change management, and operations.

Scope & Boundaries

Register

  • Business records, datasets, documents, media, work schedules, and shared knowledge.
  • Architecture designs, policies, control mappings, infrastructure code, runbooks, and decisions.

Keep in existing systems

  • Store sensitive content, credentials, and encryption keys in their approved systems. Register only metadata allowed in the registry boundary.
  • Mark backups, extracts, test copies, and analytical replicas with their source and refresh policy.

Identifiers, Tagging & Versioning

  • Use an object type prefix, a domain or owning team where useful, and a unique sequence or supported natural ID.
  • Preserve the source identifier and keep registry IDs stable across repository moves. Record aliases and successor IDs.
  • Record the owner, business domain, approved classification, retention schedule reference, lifecycle state, and authoritative link. Record when and by whom the entry was last verified.
  • Record immutable releases for approved standards, patterns, modules, and runbooks. Use the source revision or timestamp for operational records.

Create, Change & Approve

  1. Create the object in its authoritative system and assign its owner and stable ID.
  2. Register approved metadata and link supporting records by ID. Apply registry access rules to search, exports, API responses, and restricted metadata.
  3. Send changes to the domain owner and relevant architecture, security, or operations reviewers.
  4. Publish the approved revision, update dependent links, and retain the actor, timestamp, and reason for each change.

Lifecycle & Deprecation

State How to use it
Draft Available to reviewers while approval is pending
Active Current approved object or version
Deprecated Still accessible with a replacement and migration date
Superseded Replaced by a specific object ID and version
Closed or archived Retained under the applicable policy after active use ends

Record the replacement, owner, and effective date before deprecating a shared artifact. Check for applicable holds before disposition. Record retention and disposition decisions with the records owner.

Integration with Platform Engineering & IaC

  • Map landing zones to their datasets, document stores, collaboration services, and recovery requirements.
  • Link governance standards to approved Terraform modules, Ansible roles, and evidence produced by validation checks.
  • Use classification and ownership metadata to inform access controls and report coverage without exposing restricted content.
  • Link approved operational procedures and change records to the associated services.

Implementation Checklist

  1. Agree on domains, object types, owners, and source systems.
  2. Define ID, classification, retention, and versioning rules.
  3. Register critical records and link their authoritative locations.
  4. Add review and approval steps to existing change management.
  5. Review access, dangling links, stale entries, and duplicate sources. Practice resolving conflicts by routing disputed entries to the recorded owner.
  6. Schedule periodic ownership and lifecycle reviews.
